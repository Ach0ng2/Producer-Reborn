# Songwriting Craft Handbook

> **Core Principle:** Everything here is a **Guideline**, not a rigid law. Great art frequently breaks rules on purpose to create emotional impact. Keep what serves your song, discard what gets in the way.

---

## 1. Song Anatomy & Structure

Choose a common framework or modify it to fit your feel:

```text
ABABCB  Verse / Chorus / Verse / Chorus / Bridge / Chorus   (Most popular in Pop/Rock/EDM)
AABA    Verse / Verse / Bridge / Verse                     (Classic Ballad, Jazz standard)
ABAB    Alternating Verse / Chorus                         (Simple, direct)
AAA     Verse / Verse / Verse (Strophic, no chorus)        (Folk, Storytelling / Pure Rap)
```

### The 6 Building Blocks:
1. **Intro:** Sets the mood and hooks the listener within the first 5–10 seconds.
2. **Verse:** Builds the story, specific details, context, and visual world. Melodies usually sit in a lower or mid vocal register.
3. **Pre-Chorus:** Ramps up emotional tension and energy right before the Chorus explosion.
4. **Chorus:** The emotional core and main thesis of the song. The catchiest section (*earworm*) that stays in the listener's head. Usually rises to a higher melodic register.
5. **Bridge:** A plot twist, shift in perspective, fresh chord variation, or key modulation to keep the song from feeling repetitive.
6. **Outro:** The closing statement—could be a fade-out, an intimate hook repetition, or a dramatic resolution.

*Note:* Structure must serve the song's emotion, not the other way around. Never force a Bridge if going straight from Chorus 2 to the Outro already feels complete.

## 2. Rhyme, Meter, and Lyric Rhythm

### Types of Rhyme (From Strict to Flexible):
* **Perfect Rhyme:** *lean / mean*, *night / light*. (Great for punchy hooks, but overusing it can sound predictable or nursery-rhyme-like).
* **Family Rhyme:** Same vowel sound, consonants from the same phonetic family (*crate / braid*).
* **Assonance:** Same vowel sound, different consonants (*had / glass*, *home / alone*).
* **Consonance:** Same ending consonant sound, different vowels (*scene / when*).
* **Near / Slant Rhyme:** Similar-sounding words that give the ear a sense of rhyme without boxing in your vocabulary.

> **Tip:** Mix them up! Use perfect rhymes in the Chorus (for instant memorability) and slant/assonance rhymes in the Verses (so lyrics sound natural and conversational).

### Internal Rhyme:
Placing rhymes **inside a single line**, not just at the end.  
*Example:* *"Behind the **glow** of what we **know**, there's nowhere left to go..."* $\rightarrow$ Creates natural rhythmic *groove* when sung.

### Meter & Stressed Syllables:
* Singability depends less on raw syllable count and more on **where the stressed syllables land**.
* The ultimate test: **Speak or sing the lyrics out loud over the beat.** If you stumble over a phrase, trim or rearrange the syllables.
* Intentionally breaking meter (*syncopation*) can create a memorable rhythmic surprise.

## 3. Dynamics & Emotional Arc

A great song is a journey, not a flat highway.

### Energy Mapping (1 – 10 Scale):
* **Intro:** 2–3
* **Verse 1:** 4–5
* **Pre-Chorus:** 6–7
* **Chorus 1:** 8
* **Verse 2:** 5–6 (retain some momentum from Chorus 1)
* **Chorus 2:** 8–9
* **Bridge:** Dynamic shift (drop to 2–3 for intimacy, or surge to 9)
* **Final Chorus:** 9–10 (climactic peak)
* **Outro:** Resolution / decay

### Contrast Techniques:
* **Whisper to Roar:** Whispering before belting feels 10x more powerful than belting from bar one.
* **Sparse vs. Dense:** Keep the Verse stripped back (e.g., just beat + subtle bass), then bring in the full arrangement when the Chorus hits.
* **Silence is an Instrument:** A half-beat of dead air right before the Chorus drop is often the hardest-hitting moment in a track.

## 4. Writing Lyrics That Breathe

### Show, Don't Tell:
* *Flat (Tell):* "I miss you so much and I feel really sad alone."
* *Vivid (Show):* "Your jacket's still hanging behind the door, two cold mugs sitting by the sink."
* *Exception:* A blunt, unfiltered statement can carry raw emotional power if placed at the emotional climax.

### The Hook:
* The line people remember, hum, and replay after the track ends.
* Usually the song title or strongest punchline.
* Best placement: **The first line or the very last line of the Chorus.**

### Prosody (Marrying Lyrics & Music):
* **Stable Emotions (Certainty, Peace, Triumph):** Pair with melodies resolving on the root note, tonic chord resolutions, and clean perfect rhymes.
* **Unstable Emotions (Doubt, Longing, Heartbreak, Anxiety):** Pair with unresolved melodic tensions (7ths or 9ths), suspended/unresolved chords, and slant rhymes.

### Avoid:
* **Empty Clichés:** Skip overused metaphors unless you bring a fresh twist.
* **Yoda Syntax for Rhymes:** Twisting normal sentence word order just to force an end-rhyme—listeners spot it immediately.
* **Flat Energy:** Never let your Verse and Chorus share the exact same instrumental density and melodic range.

---

## 5. Vocal Phrasing for Melody

* **Vowels on Sustained Notes:** When holding a long note (2–4 beats), land on open vowel sounds (**ah, oh, ay**) so the vocal rings out cleanly rather than choking on closed consonants.
* **Monosyllabic Words:** Short single-syllable words make fast or rhythmic vocal passages easier to articulate.
* **Breathing Room:** Leave pockets of space in your phrasing so the vocalist can breathe and instruments can answer (*call and response*).

## 6. Beat-First Producer Workflow

Designed for producers who start from beats or loops:

1. **Find the Core Vibe & Hook:** Once your beat/loop is rolling in your DAW, identify 1 core emotional phrase or feeling.
2. **Scat Singing / Melody First:** Hum or sing nonsense syllables (*na-na-na* or *da-da-da*) over the beat to lock in an infectious melodic contour before forcing words.
3. **Anchor Key Words on Downbeats:** Place the most important title/hook words on strong downbeats in the Chorus.
4. **Build Verses for Context:** Fill the Verses with concrete imagery, storytelling, time, and place.
5. **Singability Check:** Perform the lyrics at full tempo. Trim any tongue-twisting syllables immediately.
6. **Arrangement Tweaks:** Match your DAW arrangement to the lyrical arc (mute layers in Verses, automate filters and add layers for Choruses).
