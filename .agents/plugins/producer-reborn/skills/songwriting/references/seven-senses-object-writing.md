# Seven-Senses "Object Writing" Guide

Based on Pat Pattison's *Object Writing* methodology taught at Berklee College of Music. Instead of telling listeners how to feel using abstract clichés (*"I'm heartbroken"*), you immerse them in a concrete moment using **all seven senses**. The brain retains vivid sensory imagery far longer than abstract emotion—forcing the listener to step inside the scene and feel the emotion themselves.

---

## 1. The Seven Senses

Traditional writing stops at five senses. Effective songwriting adds **two internal & physical senses** that make lyrics visceral:

1. **Sight** — Visual details, colors, light, posture, expressions (*"the taillights bleeding through rain on the windshield"*).
2. **Hearing** — Background music, ambient noise, tone of voice, silence (*"a scratchy AM radio station playing old Memphis blues"*).
3. **Smell** — Air, weather, perfume, smoke, rooms (*"wet asphalt and stale cedarwood"*).
4. **Taste** — Drinks, bitterness, salt, metallic air (*"warm bourbon neat in a plastic cup"*).
5. **Touch** — Texture, temperature, skin contact, weight (*"cold porcelain against your fingertips"*).
6. **Organic** — **Internal bodily sensations**: heart pounding, stomach dropping, chest tightening, throat burning, breath catching.
7. **Kinesthetic** — **Body movement & spatial balance**: pacing the hallway, hands shaking, leaning in, two-stepping across hardwood, stumbling.

> **Language-Agnostic Rule:** Concrete imagery works in every language. Specificity beats fancy vocabulary every single time.

---

## 2. The 10-Minute Object Writing Exercise

Run this ritual before opening your rhyme dictionary or melody pad:

### Step 1: Anchor on One Specific Moment
Write one concrete moment at the top of the page. **Never pick a vague topic.**
- ❌ *Too vague:* "A breakup" or "Missing someone"
- ✅ *Specific moment:* "Watching them pack the last cardboard box in the hallway at 2 AM"

### Step 2: Split the Page & Brainstorm (10-Minute Timer)
List all 7 senses on the left. Set a **10-minute timer** and write raw, unfiltered sensory details on the right.
- **Do NOT try to rhyme.**
- **Do NOT fix grammar or sound poetic.** Just capture concrete objects, textures, sounds, and physical sensations.

### Step 3: Circle the 2–3 Hardest-Hitting Details
When the timer ends, scan your list and circle the **2 or 3 details** that hit hardest emotionally—especially combinations that layer **Movement (Kinesthetic) + Visual (Sight) + Visceral (Taste/Organic/Touch)**.
- **Chorus Blueprint:** Stack those 2–3 circled anchors together into your Chorus hook.
- **Verse Blueprint:** Use the remaining sensory details to ground Verse 1 and Verse 2 in real time and space.

---

## 3. Why Layering Senses Creates Sticky Hooks

- *Abstract (Forgettable):* "My heart aches because you moved on without me."
- *Sensory Stack (Sticky):* Combining `[Kinesthetic movement]` + `[Visual expression]` + `[Taste/Solitude]` puts a movie camera directly inside the listener's head—making the Chorus memorable without ever naming the emotion explicitly.
